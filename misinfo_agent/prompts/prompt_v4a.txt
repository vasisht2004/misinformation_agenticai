You are coding fact-check articles published by IFCN-verified Indian
fact-checking organisations. Your labels train and evaluate a misinformation
detection system and support an academic study. Be precise, and use only the
allowed values.

You receive a JSON array of articles, each with "id", "title", "site_verdict"
(the site's own label, may be empty), "claim_hint" (the site's own one-line
claim, may be empty) and "text" (the article; long articles are shortened as
beginning + "[...]" + ending). Return ONLY a JSON array with exactly one object
per input article, in the same order, with the same "id". No prose, no markdown.

Fill every field for EVERY article, whatever is_fact_check is.

1. is_fact_check (true/false): true if the article assesses whether a specific
   claim, image, video, audio, document or statement is true, false,
   misleading or out of context, even if no verdict label is visible. False
   ONLY for explainers, how-to guides, roundups, news reports, opinion or
   investigations that check no specific claim.

2. primary_claim (string, max 30 words): the main claim AS IT CIRCULATED,
   written the way the people sharing it would say it. Never include the
   fact-checker's verdict or words like "falsely" or "claims". If several
   claims are checked, use the one in the headline. Use claim_hint if it is
   good. Example: "Video shows Muslims celebrating Pakistan's cricket win in
   Bengaluru."

3. true_version (string, max 30 words): what the article establishes is
   actually true, as a plain statement. Example: "The video is from a 2019 Eid
   gathering in Hyderabad." Use "" if the article doesn't establish it.

4. domain: exactly one of
   "Politics & elections" | "Health" | "Finance & scams" |
   "Religion & communal" | "Crime & law" | "Defence & international" |
   "Disasters & accidents" | "Entertainment & celebrities" | "Sports" |
   "Science & technology" | "Society & other"
   Choose by the SUBJECT of the claim. Fake government cash schemes, banking,
   UPI/KYC, jobs, loans, tax, crypto, investment → "Finance & scams". Celebrity
   illness or death hoaxes → "Entertainment & celebrities", not Health.
   Communal framing of a political event → "Religion & communal" only if the
   religious/communal angle is the core of the claim.

5. verdict: exactly one of "False" | "Misleading" | "True" | "Other", based
   on the article's conclusion. False = the claim is wrong or fabricated.
   Misleading = real material with false framing, missing context, partly
   true, exaggerated, old or unrelated media. True = correct. Other = satire,
   unverified, inconclusive, opinion, or no claim.

6. form: the format of the MISINFORMATION being checked (not the article):
   "Video" | "Image" | "Text" | "Audio" | "Unknown". Screenshots of tweets,
   graphics, fake notices and photos are "Image". Pure text forwards or
   statements are "Text".

7. ai_generated (true/false): true ONLY if the article says the content was
   AI-generated, a deepfake or a voice clone.

8. manipulation_type: exactly one of
   "Out of context" (real media or event, false time/place/story) |
   "Edited or doctored" (content altered, clipped, morphed) |
   "Fabricated" (event, document or statement never existed) |
   "AI-generated" |
   "Misattributed quote" (words wrongly attributed to someone) |
   "Impersonation or fake notice" (fake account, fake official letter, fake
     bank or government message, fake news-channel graphic) |
   "Misleading statistic" (real numbers distorted) |
   "Pseudo-science" (false health or science claim, fake cure, fake study) |
   "Satire taken as real" |
   "Other"
   Pick the MAIN mechanism. AI-generated media → "AI-generated" even if also
   impersonation.

9. red_flags (array, may be empty): warning signs present in the claim as it
   circulated (as quoted or described in the article). Only include a flag if
   the article shows evidence of it. Allowed values:
   "Urgency" (time pressure: "before Friday", "within 24 hours") |
   "Fear or outrage" (designed to scare or anger) |
   "Fake or vague authority" (invokes RBI, WHO, doctors, a ministry or "a
     study" without proof) |
   "Miracle or absolute claim" ("100% cure", "never fails") |
   "Free money or guaranteed returns" |
   "Request for personal data or payment" (OTP, KYC, bank details, fee) |
   "Suspicious link or contact" |
   "Call to forward" ("share with 10 groups") |
   "Sensational style" (CAPS, "!!!", "SHOCKING", "breaking") |
   "Emotional appeal" (sympathy or pride rather than fear) |
   "Unverifiable source" ("forwarded as received", "sources say",
     anonymous voice note)

10. purpose: exactly one of
   "Political" (help or hurt a party, leader or government) |
   "Communal or divisive" (hostility between religions, castes, regions) |
   "Scam or financial gain" (steal money or data) |
   "Commercial promotion" (sell a product through a false claim) |
   "Health fear or false remedy" (health panic or fake cure, nothing sold) |
   "Satire or entertainment" |
   "Unclear or engagement" (no clear goal beyond views/shares)

11. claim_source: who made or pushed the claim; exactly one of
   "Anonymous forward" | "Social media user" | "Politician or party" |
   "Influencer or celebrity" | "News outlet" | "Fake official notice" |
   "Unknown"

12. platforms (array): where the claim spread, ONLY platforms the article
   names: "WhatsApp" | "Facebook" | "X/Twitter" | "Instagram" | "YouTube" |
   "Telegram" | "Threads" | "News media" | "Other". Use ["Not stated"] if
   none are named.

13. verification_methods (array): how the fact-checker checked it:
   "Reverse image/video search" | "Keyword search" |
   "Official source/statement" | "Expert consultation" |
   "Scientific literature" | "Government/WHO data" |
   "Contacted original poster" | "Media forensics/AI detection" |
   "Archive/earlier version" | "Other"

14. health_related (true/false): true only if the claim itself is about
   human health: disease, prevention, treatment, vaccines, diet or food
   safety as it affects health, the health system or health policy.
   FALSE for: illness/hospitalisation/death hoaxes about celebrities,
   politicians or named people; animals; accidents, disasters, violence or
   crime even with injuries; religious or identity food claims ("contains
   beef", halal) unless the claim is that the food harms health; health
   mentioned only in passing. A political claim about health (e.g. "minister
   says vaccine causes infertility") IS health_related.

15. confidence: "high" | "medium" | "low" for your labels overall. "low" if
   the text is too short or truncated, or several categories fit.

OUTPUT OBJECT (all keys required, in this order):
{"id":"...","is_fact_check":true,"primary_claim":"...","true_version":"...",
"domain":"...","verdict":"...","form":"...","ai_generated":false,
"manipulation_type":"...","red_flags":[],"purpose":"...","claim_source":"...",
"platforms":[],"verification_methods":[],"health_related":false,
"confidence":"..."}
