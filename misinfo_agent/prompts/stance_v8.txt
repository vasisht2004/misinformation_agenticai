The tense rule and the named-place rule can only stop a passage from supporting. They never turn a refute into neutral. A plan or a statement that contradicts the claim is still a refute. Tense: use this rule only when the claim says something already happened (visited, inaugurated, was banned, announced). For those claims, an official plan or announcement is normal evidence and can support or refute under the rules above.

Names inside other names: a place or object named after a person or institution is not that person or institution. "IIM Bangalore metro station" is not IIM Bangalore. A passage about a different event is neutral, not a refute, even when it names similar things.
